\newcommand{\GeneMapText}{\emph{Do false edges look like biology?} Mantis also grouped the 424 genes into \McLeaves{} leaf clusters by their UniProt or NCBI function text alone, which contains no analysis result (Appendix~\ref{app:mantis}). With this independent partition we asked the question co-expression studies use for validation, whether edges join genes of related function (post hoc, REV4 Amendment~4). In the null, where no pair co-varies between donors, \McModNullFrac{} of pseudobulk's false edges joined two genes of the same cluster, against \McModBase{} of all pairs, and the degree-adjusted modularity of the partition on the false edges lay $z=\McModNullZ$ standard deviations above 10{,}000 random relabellings of the genes in the three subtypes ($p\le\McModNullP$ each; Fig.~\ref{fig:anatomy}f). Real-donor networks were similarly or less coherent (pseudobulk $z=\McModRealPBZ$; XDS-M \McModRealXZ), and the calls on which the two methods disagree were not coherent (pseudobulk-only $z=\McModPBonlyZ$; XDS-M-only \McModXonlyZ). A functional-enrichment check would therefore not tell the false network from the real one. This is what the leak predicts: it carries within-cell programs, whose genes tend to share function, to the donor level (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}). The clearest instance is the Homeo\_CECR2 cluster Mantis labelled \emph{\McTopLabel} (Fig.~\ref{fig:anatomy}e), whose \McTopN{} heat-shock genes the panel includes a priori as controls for a within-cell stress program that is also a known processing artifact \citep{vandenBrink2017,Marsh2022artifact}: it has the highest median null false-edge degree (\McTopNull, against \McMedNull{} over all genes), its pairs have median within-donor correlation \RsixHSrw{} (all pairs \RsixAllrw), and \RsixHSpct{} of them were false calls (all pairs \RsixAllpct). This particular cluster does not stand out in the other subtypes (rank \McRepRank), the coherence is weaker at Mantis's coarser 12-cluster level (null $z=\McModNullZcoarse$), and the pre-specified test of pseudobulk-only degree across clusters was negative ($p=\McKWPBonly$; Appendix~\ref{app:mantis}).}

\newcommand{\CalibText}{Table~\ref{tab:calib} gives the run-level error of every test. From 200 pseudo-donors up, pseudobulk and LCP made false calls in every run, and LCPd and single-split XDS in 14--32\% of runs. XDS-M made at least one false call in 3--8\% of runs from 100 pseudo-donors up (\RtwoPctAllXDSM{} at full size) and stayed within the frozen bound in \XDSMholdsLarge{} subtype--size and blood settings, failing only ADAM\_GPNMB at 400 pseudo-donors. The bound rejects an exactly calibrated method in about 3\% of settings, so one narrow failure is compatible with nominal error, but the exact 95\% upper limits reach \RfUpperPooled{} for the pooled 400-donor runs and \RfUpperThree{} for ADAM\_GPNMB at 400. Below 100 donors XDS-M's normal reference failed (49\% of runs at 25 pseudo-donors), and KX, which is within bound from 200 donors on the donor-centred null, failed at small sizes and on the pooled null in ADAM\_GPNMB (\RfKXpoolThree{} of 100 runs).

\emph{Small cohorts.} With $10^5$ permutations the smallest attainable $p$-value exceeds the first BH threshold among 89{,}000 pairs ($5.7\times10^{-7}$), so a permutation test calls only if at least 18 pairs tie at the floor, which LCP rarely reached at 25 donors (\RfFloorLCPTwentyFive{} pairs per run); our earlier small-cohort runs were therefore uninformative. With $2\times10^6$ permutations (REV4 R1; at least $m/q$), LCP made false calls in \RfLCPTwentyFive{}, \RfLCPFifty{} and \RfLCPHundred{} runs at 25, 50 and 100 pseudo-donors and LCPd in \RfLCPdTwentyFive{}, \RfLCPdFifty{} and \RfLCPdHundred, whereas permutation-based XDS made false calls in \RfXDSpermTwentyFive{}, \RfXDSpermFifty{} and \RfXDSpermHundred{} runs (exact 95\% upper limits \RfXDSpermUpTwentyFive, \RfXDSpermUpFifty{} and \RfXDSpermUpHundred), within the pre-specified rule of at most 2 of 20 runs in \RfXDSpermTwentyFiveHolds, \RfXDSpermFiftyHolds{} and \RfXDSpermHundredHolds{} of three subtypes (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:R1}). Permutation-based XDS was thus the only test not rejected at 25 and 50 donors, at very low power (Section~\ref{sec:sim}).

\emph{Batches.} On the brain-bank-stratified null XDS-M made false calls in \RfBankXDSM{} runs, outside the bound in two subtypes, and every other test failed in at least one subtype (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:R2}). These pseudo-donors are not exactly null: donor-centring shrinks few-cell donors' effects, so part of each bank's mean survives (median shift \RfBankShift{} SD) as covariance shared within a bank. Centring the design-standardized values within bank before forming the products (XDS-Mb; post hoc, REV4 Amendment~2) reduced the runs with a call to \RfBankXDSMb{}, within the bound in every subtype, whereas bank-centred pseudobulk still made \RfBankPBb{} false calls per run. A pre-specified check (Amendment~3) confirmed the explanation: when each bank's mean expression was divided out before the bank-stratified reassignment, XDS-M made false calls in \BmX{} runs, within the bound in every subtype, and pseudobulk \BmPB{} per run. Known batches should therefore be centred out, as AD groups are in Section~\ref{sec:dc}.

\emph{Covariates.} Real cohorts violate (A3): cell number differs between AD cases and controls (Section~\ref{sec:dc}). XDS-M extends to donor covariates by residualizing each split's design-standardized half-donor values on them before forming the products (XDS-Mc; sex, age, brain bank, AD status and ethnicity, \CoRealP{} columns with the intercept; REV4 Amendment~3). Theorem~\ref{thm:xdsm} does not cover the residualization, so its calibration here is empirical. On the donor-centred null with each pseudo-donor carrying a real donor's covariates, XDS-Mc made a false call in \CoNullXc{} runs, within the pre-specified bound in every subtype, whereas covariate-adjusted pseudobulk still made \CoNullPBc{} false calls per run.}
\newcommand{\RecText}{With about 100 donors or more, XDS-M was the most reliable test we found (KX matched it only on the donor-centred null from 200 donors): 3--8\% of runs with any false call across cohort sizes and tissues, for genes above about 0.1 UMI per cell and with known batches centred out, at some cost in power (\XDSMLpow{} against 0.98 for LCP at 200 simulated donors with a median of 20 cells). Below about 100 donors, XDS with permutation $p$-values and at least $m/q$ permutations ($m$ pairs, level $q$) was the only test not rejected, at low power, and no test we evaluated was both calibrated and powerful; this is feasible for targeted gene sets (about \RfRoneMin{} minutes per run on one GPU for 89{,}000 pairs) but needs tail approximations \citep{Knijnenburg2009} at transcriptome scale. For differential co-expression, centre the unstandardized products within groups (XDS-Mgu). Functional enrichment of the edges is not evidence that they are between-donor signal.}
