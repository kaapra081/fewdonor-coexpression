TITLE
Between-donor gene co-expression from few donors: a leak-corrected permutation test and a bound on what any test can detect

ABSTRACT
In multi-donor single-cell RNA-seq, two genes can co-vary within donors, reflecting cell-state programs, or between donors, reflecting donor-level variation such as genotype, environment or disease. Between-donor co-expression is usually tested by correlating pseudobulk donor means. We show that this targets the wrong quantity. Under a Poisson measurement model, the covariance of donor ratio-means equals the between-donor covariance plus the within-donor covariance scaled by each donor's inverse cell count, so cell-state programs leak into pseudobulk and are reported as donor-level associations. We give an unbiased count-scale correction for this leak, estimated from each donor's own cells, and prove an information bound for testing one gene pair: with D donors, no test has more local power than an oracle that observes the true donor means of only D_eff = sum_d rho_dj rho_dk donors, where rho is the donor-specific reliability of a donor mean. At about 40 cells per donor, D_eff is roughly one third of D, which quantifies why few-donor designs cannot resolve pair-level between-donor structure and yields a donors-versus-cells design rule. We propose LCP, a leak-corrected, precision-standardized donor-permutation test. In simulations with exact ground truth, LCP controls the false discovery rate from 24 donors upward and matches the power of a noise-limited oracle benchmark. Pseudobulk correlation has a false discovery rate of 0.13-0.14, and an existing moment-corrected test has almost no power. We also report two negative results: at 16 or fewer donors no test based on observed donor means, ours included, is calibrated in the tail used for multiple-testing correction, and covariance intervals undercover even when computed from the true donor means. We apply LCP to microglia from two Alzheimer's disease cohorts (SEA-AD, 84 donors; Lee et al. 2026, 80 donors) and evaluate calibration by donor subsampling and replication across cohorts.
