TITLE
Between-donor gene co-expression in multi-donor single-cell data: a leak-corrected permutation test and the limits of per-pair inference

ABSTRACT
In multi-donor single-cell RNA-seq, two genes can co-vary within donors, reflecting cell-state programs, or between donors, reflecting genetic, environmental, disease or technical variation. Between-donor co-expression is usually tested by correlating pseudobulk donor means. With cells sampled independently within donors, the covariance of donor ratio-means is the between-donor covariance plus the within-donor covariance scaled by each donor's effective inverse cell count. This is a count-scale instance of the classical multilevel (BCES-type) measurement-error decomposition, and it means that cell-state programs leak into pseudobulk. We estimate the leak for each donor from count moments. In a Gaussian working model with known sampling variances and no leak, a test that uses only the two genes' donor means has at most the local power of an oracle with D_eff = sum_d rho_dj rho_dk perfectly measured donors, where rho is the reliability of a donor mean; for 417 genes in SEA-AD and Lee et al. 2026 microglia, the median D_eff/D is 0.90-0.98 at the cohorts' cell numbers but 0.53 at 40 cells per donor. We propose LCP, a leak-corrected, precision-standardized donor-permutation test of independence of donor effects, and LCP2, which also accounts for the variance of the leak estimate by a within-donor jackknife. On real nuclei with exact truth, made by reassigning the nuclei of two SEA-AD microglial supertypes that have naturally few nuclei per donor to pseudo-donors with the real cell numbers (median 15 and 43 per donor), pseudobulk made 2,714-3,714 false discoveries per dataset on average and LCP and LCP2 none. In five held-out simulation designs with 24-40 donors, LCP's false discovery rate (FDR) was 0.041-0.050, against 0.08-0.30 for pseudobulk, at 0.89-1.04 times the power of a noise-matched oracle. LCP is not calibrated at 16 donors with 40 cells or at 15 cells per donor; in new simulations of those designs LCP2's FDR was 0.052 and 0.025 (LCP: 0.110 and 0.077), at 0.87 and 0.96 of LCP's power at 24 and 40 donors and 0.75 with 15 cells per donor. Post hoc ablations show that standardization and permutation remove most of pseudobulk's FDR excess, while the leak term keeps the type-I error for within-donor-only pairs near 0.05 (0.11-0.21 without it). LCP is not calibrated when sequencing depth is coupled to cell state (FDR 0.22 in a stress design), and coupling of similar size is present in both cohorts; it tests independence rather than zero covariance (weak-null type-I error 0.06-0.10), and covariance intervals undercover even on true donor means. At the cohorts' real cell numbers the leak is under 1% of the pseudobulk covariance among pseudobulk calls and the methods largely agree; in a naturally rare supertype they disagree on 39% of pseudobulk calls and change half of the network's top-20 hub genes.
